THE FEDERAL STANDARD

Start with the requirements
that apply to your ICT.

Section 508 addresses information and communication technology developed, procured, maintained, or used by U.S. federal agencies. For suppliers, the solicitation and contract define the accessibility requirements for the ICT being delivered.

CORE TECHNICAL BASELINEWCAG 2.0
Levels A & AA

The Revised Section 508 Standards also include requirements beyond WCAG. The evaluation depends on the product, its functionality, and applicable scoping provisions.

Understand WCAG conformance

Documents & multimedia

Evaluate the applicable requirements for PDFs, office documents, and media. Include support documentation where relevant.

Electronic content + support documentation

Authoring tools & other ICT

Identify additional needs for tools, hardware, or specialist interfaces. Confirm testing coverage as part of scoping.

Functionality determines coverage

The scope comes first. Native-app testing, hardware, and specialist ICT coverage are agreed before the engagement. Section 255 telecommunications requirements are considered separately when relevant. Agency officials determine applicable exceptions.

YOUR TECHNICAL HANDOFF

Clear findings.
Useful documentation.

Depending on the engagement, your project can include the following deliverables.

  • Accessibility assessment report

    Findings by page, feature, or document, connected to the relevant WCAG or Section 508 criteria and their effect on users.

  • Prioritized remediation plan

    A practical order for addressing barriers, with recommendations based on impact and implementation needs.

  • Accessibility Conformance Report

    A completed VPAT®-based report for the agreed product, with applicable criteria, conformance levels, and meaningful explanations.

  • Implementation guidance

    Technical recommendations and relevant code examples to help your developers resolve the issues accurately.

  • Verification summary

    A record of the changes tested, the methods used, results, and any issues that remain unresolved.

  • Code-level remediation

    When implementation is included, corrections within the website or product—not just a list of problems to address.

UNDERSTANDING THE ACR

More than a checkbox.
Explain the result.

The VPAT® is ITI’s reporting template. The completed document is an Accessibility Conformance Report: a record of how the evaluated product supports the relevant criteria.

For federal ICT, use an edition that includes the Revised Section 508 Standards, such as the 508 edition or the International edition when additional standards are needed.

Good remarks describe the tested functionality, explain gaps, and make the scope clear. A remediation plan provides context; a planned fix does not change the product’s current result.

SECTION 508 FAQ

Your questions.
Clearer next steps.

About federal requirements, procurement, testing, and documentation.

Read the official ACR/VPAT FAQ.

Is Section 508 compliance mandatory for private websites?

Section 508 directly governs federal agencies’ ICT. It does not automatically cover every private website, or every website owned by a federal contractor.

A supplier may need to meet Section 508 requirements for ICT delivered under a federal contract. Review the solicitation, contract, and product scope. Other laws or funding requirements may also apply; federal funding alone should not be treated as proof that Section 508 covers the site.

Do we need a Section 508 VPAT to sell to the federal government?

Expect to provide an Accessibility Conformance Report for ICT procurement. Federal buyers use it to evaluate accessibility against the requirements in their solicitation.

The VPAT® is a common way to create that report, but the template itself is not the only reporting format. Confirm the buyer’s requested edition, scope, and format before preparing the ACR.

What if our product isn’t fully accessible? Will the government still consider it?

An accurate ACR can disclose gaps and allow a buyer to evaluate the product. It should describe current support honestly, with remediation plans clearly separated from completed work.

That does not guarantee acceptance. The agency evaluates its requirements, available products, and any applicable exceptions or best-meets provisions. Those determinations belong to the agency, not the vendor or testing provider.

What about legal liability or interpretations?

Equal Access provides digital accessibility services, not legal advice. Our work is technical: testing, remediation, verification, and documentation.

Discuss legal obligations with qualified counsel and procurement or program questions with the responsible agency officials. An ACR is not a guarantee of legal protection.

How often should we update our Section 508 documentation?

Keep the report aligned with the product version and evaluated state. Releases, bug fixes, content changes, or new functionality may require retesting and an updated ACR.

Review significant changes before reusing an older report. EqualAccess. can support continued checks through Ongoing Monitoring & Support.

Can you help us actually fix the issues you find?

Yes. When remediation is included, we can implement code-level corrections or work alongside your developers and agency. Technology coverage is confirmed during scoping.

After the changes, we retest the affected functionality and document the results. Explore Code-Level Remediation.
