WHAT IS IN SCOPE?

Access to a service goes beyond the main website.

For organizations subject to HHS’s rule, covered digital experiences can include web content and mobile apps provided directly or through contractual, licensing, or other arrangements. That makes it important to look beyond the homepage.

  • Public websites

    Service information, provider directories, navigation, forms, and interactive functionality.

  • Appointment and intake journeys

    Scheduling, registration, applications, and other workflows used to access a program.

  • Patient- or participant-facing platforms

    Experiences used to access your services, including relevant third-party systems.

  • Electronic documents

    PDFs and other documents used to apply for, access, or participate in programs and services.

  • Mobile experiences

    Where apps form part of the scope, we help identify the testing requirement and determine the appropriate next step.

  • Third-party technology

    Vendor, contracted, or licensed functionality that forms part of the covered experience.

A vendor does not automatically remove the accessibility issue.

We can help identify technical barriers and vendor dependencies. Your legal team determines how the requirements apply to the arrangement.

For example, the pre-existing-document exception does not apply to documents currently used to apply for, gain access to, or participate in a recipient’s programs. Scope depends on the facts; your advisers determine applicability.

Accessibility Audits & Testing

Start with an evidence-based picture of your digital experience. We combine automated analysis with expert manual evaluation of agreed pages, components, and user journeys.

HOW IT WORKS

From uncertainty to
documented technical progress.

One consistent five-step process, scoped around your systems, your responsibilities, and the work you need us to perform.

  1. 01

    Assess

    Map the websites, journeys, documents, and vendor systems in scope. Establish ownership, requirements, and detectable barriers.

  2. 02

    Manual Testing

    Experts evaluate real interactions and assistive-technology behavior, reproduce barriers, and prioritize findings.

  3. 03

    Code-Level Remediation

    Make agreed website and content corrections directly, with your developers, or alongside the responsible vendors.

  4. 04

    Retest & Verify

    Repeat the affected journeys and check the fixes against the relevant criteria and agreed testing methods.

  5. 05

    Documentation

    Record what was tested, corrected, and verified, together with remaining limitations and dependencies.

HHS’s Section 504 regulation applies to programs or activities receiving federal financial assistance from the Department of Health and Human Services, including assistance received through another recipient. Your legal or compliance team should determine applicability to your organization.

EqualAccess. focuses on the technical digital accessibility work. Read the regulation’s application provision.

Under the current HHS regulation, recipients with 15 or more employees must meet the specified web/mobile WCAG requirements beginning May 11, 2027. Recipients with fewer than 15 employees have until May 10, 2028.

HHS extended the original dates by one year in May 2026. The extension does not suspend other Section 504 obligations. Read the HHS update.

The current HHS rule incorporates WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA success criteria and conformance requirements for covered web content and mobile apps.

WCAG 2.2 adds newer criteria and removes the obsolete parsing criterion. We can scope broader WCAG 2.2 AA testing while retaining the mapping to WCAG 2.1 relevant to HHS’s rule. HHS requirement · W3C’s WCAG 2.2 explanation.
