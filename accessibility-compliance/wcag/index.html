THE TERMINOLOGY

“Compliance” and “conformance”
have different jobs.

You’ll often hear the terms used interchangeably. In a WCAG evaluation, the distinction helps keep technical findings and legal questions clear.

WCAG compliance

Common buyer language for efforts to meet WCAG accessibility requirements, often discussed alongside organizational policies, procurement, or legal obligations.

WCAG is a technical standard. Meeting it does not determine whether an organization has satisfied every law or regulation that may apply.

TESTFIX
VERIFY

WCAG conformance

The term WCAG uses for satisfying its defined success criteria and conformance requirements at a claimed level.

A clean automated scan, or fixing most identified issues, does not establish conformance. The complete requirements and the scope of the claim matter.

The practical goal: understand what’s failing, fix the barriers, verify the result, and document the work accurately.

W3C guidance on understanding conformance.

THE STANDARD

Four principles.
One more accessible experience.

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines are developed through W3C’s Web Accessibility Initiative. Their testable success criteria are organized around Perceivable, Operable, Understandable, and Robust.

PPerceivable

Can people perceive the content?

Information should not depend on a single sense or presentation. Consider text alternatives, captions, adaptable structure, contrast, and content that remains usable when resized.

Alternative text · Captions · Contrast · Reflow · Structure

OOperable

Can people operate the interface?

People should be able to navigate and use functionality without relying exclusively on a mouse. Keyboard access, focus, timing, pointer interactions, and navigation all matter.

Keyboard access · Focus · Navigation · Target size

UUnderstandable

Can people understand what happens?

Content and controls should behave predictably. Clear instructions, helpful errors, consistent patterns, and accessible authentication help people understand and complete tasks.

Labels · Instructions · Errors · Predictable behavior

RRobust

Can assistive technology understand it?

Content and controls need to expose useful information to browsers and assistive technologies. Semantic HTML and accurate names, roles, states, and values are central.

Semantic HTML · Names · Roles · States

Read the W3C WCAG overview.

CONFORMANCE LEVELS

Three levels.
They build on one another.

Level AA includes Level A. Level AAA includes both A and AA, plus additional requirements. They describe cumulative conformance requirements, rather than quality scores.

For most EqualAccess. website engagements, Levels A and AA are the starting point unless a different target is required.

W3C does not recommend requiring AAA as a general policy for entire sites, because some content cannot satisfy every AAA criterion.

W3C guidance on conformance levels.

  1. A

    Foundational requirements

    The applicable Level A success criteria.

  2. AA

    A common evaluation target

    All Level A and AA success criteria, with the conformance requirements.

  3. AAA

    Additional requirements

    All Level A, AA, and AAA success criteria, with the conformance requirements.

WCAG VERSIONS

Which version should you use?

Begin with the requirements governing your project. WCAG 2.0, 2.1, and 2.2 remain existing standards; newer WCAG 2 versions add requirements rather than invalidating earlier versions.

WCAG 2.0

Established the core WCAG 2 framework and success criteria. It remains referenced in requirements including the Revised Section 508 Standards.

WCAG 2.1

Added criteria particularly relevant to mobile accessibility, low vision, and cognitive and learning disabilities. The DOJ’s Title II web and mobile rule uses 2.1 AA.

WCAG 2.2

Adds criteria for focus not being obscured, dragging, target size, consistent help, redundant entry, and accessible authentication.

W3C encourages the latest version

Free to choose? WCAG 2.2 is a strong target for new work. If a law, contract, procurement requirement, or customer specifies a version and level, test against that required target and document the mapping.

WCAG 2 versions are designed for backward compatibility. One specific change is the removal of obsolete 4.1.1 Parsing in 2.2; W3C explains related 2.0 errata and 2.1 notes. Version guidance · What’s new in 2.2 · Section 508 baseline · DOJ Title II baseline. Sources reviewed October 4, 2026.

BEYOND INDIVIDUAL CHECKS

Conformance is more than
an isolated checklist.

WCAG defines five conformance requirements. Individual success criteria matter, but they are not the whole picture.

  1. Requirement 1

    Selected level

    For AA, satisfy the applicable Level A and AA success criteria and the conformance requirements.

  2. Requirement 2

    Complete pages

    Conformance applies to a full page, including responsive variations—not just the parts that pass.

  3. Requirement 3

    Complete processes

    Evaluate all steps in a multi-page process. An accessible product page does not establish an accessible checkout.

  4. Requirement 4

    Accessibility support

    Rely on technology used in ways that work with users’ browsers and assistive technologies.

  5. Requirement 5

    No interference

    Content not relied upon for conformance must still avoid blocking access to the rest of the experience.

HUMAN TESTING MATTERS

Automated testing finds issues.
It doesn’t tell the whole story.

Tools can quickly identify detectable problems and help monitor large websites. Other requirements depend on human judgment, context, interaction, and assistive technology behavior.

AUTOMATED

Find repeatable technical issues.

  • Certain contrast failures
  • Missing attributes
  • Some structural issues
  • Certain ARIA problems
  • Repeatable technical patterns
MANUAL

Evaluate how the experience works.

  • Meaningful alternative text
  • Logical focus order
  • Keyboard usability
  • Forms and error handling
  • Names in context and complete journeys
ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY

Check important interactions.

  • Screen reader output
  • Component announcements
  • Form behavior
  • Dynamic updates
  • Navigation and dialogs

Keyboard accessibility example

BEFORE · FINDING

Choose a delivery option

A dropdown works with a mouse but cannot be fully operated with a keyboard.

Mouse works · keyboard path blocked

Accessibility impact
A keyboard user may be unable to choose an option or finish the task.

CODE-LEVEL FIX

Correct keyboard behavior, focus management, semantics, and state communication.

Native <select>, where suitable Or implement the appropriate ARIA pattern + key behavior + accurate states.
VERIFIED · RETEST

Options can be reached and selected.

Retest keyboard operation, focus behavior, and relevant screen reader output.

Remediated and retested

Forms accessibility example

BEFORE · FINDING

Email address

An error is visible but not programmatically associated with its field.

Visible error · missing relationship

Accessibility impact
A screen reader user may not know which field needs correction or why.

CODE-LEVEL FIX

Associate the error with the field and update invalid state and feedback appropriately.

<input id="email" aria-invalid="true" aria-describedby="email-error"> <p id="email-error"> Enter a valid email. </p>
VERIFIED · RETEST

Field and error are connected.

Submit again and check that the field, error, and correction path are communicated.

Remediated and retested

Illustrative result for this issue, not a claim about a complete website.

Contrast accessibility example

BEFORE · FINDING

Important account information

Important normal-sized text falls below the applicable contrast threshold.

Contrast needs correction

Accessibility impact
People with low vision may have difficulty reading the information.

CODE-LEVEL FIX

Adjust foreground or background colors while preserving the intended hierarchy.

/* Example of an accessible pair */ color: #10213A; background: #FFFFFF; /* Check affected states too. */
VERIFIED · RETEST

Color pair and affected states retested.

Recalculate the ratio and inspect relevant states, including hover and focus.

Remediated and retested

Illustrative result for this issue, not a claim about a complete website.

Dialog accessibility example

BEFORE · FINDING

Delivery preferences

A modal opens, but focus stays behind it and its purpose is not announced.

Dialog purpose and focus unclear

Accessibility impact
Users may interact with the page behind the dialog or miss the dialog content.

CODE-LEVEL FIX

Correct naming, semantics, initial focus, focus containment, and focus return.

<dialog aria-labelledby="title"> <h2 id="title"> Delivery preferences </h2> <!-- controls and close action --> </dialog>
VERIFIED · RETEST

Dialog purpose and focus behavior retested.

Retest with keyboard and relevant assistive technology, including closing the dialog.

Remediated and retested

ACCURATE CLAIMS MATTER

A 95% score is not
WCAG conformance.

Scores, issue counts, and health indicators can help track progress. They are not conformance levels.

A conformance claim depends on the applicable success criteria, all conformance requirements, and the defined scope and level. It does not follow from reaching a scanner score.

EqualAccess. separates progress metrics from claims about what has been evaluated and established.

W3C guidance on conformance claims.

W3C overview.

W3C conformance guidance.

WCAG stands for Web Content Accessibility Guidelines. It is an international accessibility standard developed through W3C’s Web Accessibility Initiative, with testable success criteria intended to make web content more accessible to people with disabilities.

“WCAG compliance” is common language for meeting WCAG accessibility requirements. “Conformance” is the terminology WCAG uses for satisfying its defined success criteria and conformance requirements at a particular level.

WCAG 2.2 Level AA conformance requires the applicable Level A and AA success criteria, along with the conformance requirements. Level AA includes Level A; it is not a separate set of checks that can establish conformance independently.
