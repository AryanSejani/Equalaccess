Plan around the right deadline.

Current DOJ web and mobile rule compliance dates.

April 26, 2027

Public entities with a total population of 50,000 or more, excluding special district governments.

April 26, 2028

Public entities with a total population below 50,000, and all special district governments.

The April 2026 interim final rule extended these dates. Population follows the rule’s definitions, rather than employee, student, or website-user counts. School districts are not special district governments. Read DOJ’s deadline guidance · 28 CFR § 35.200. Sources reviewed October 4, 2026.

WHO THE RULE COVERS

Public services.
Open to your whole community.

ADA Title II applies to state and local public entities. The web and mobile rule covers digital content and applications they provide directly or through contractual, licensing, or other arrangements.

That can include benefit applications, permit forms, payments, booking journeys, program information, and relevant online documents.

Using a vendor does not remove the public entity’s responsibility. Include outsourced portals and apps in your accessibility planning. Independent third-party posts have a different, limited exception.

DOJ’s overview of covered public entities · Web and mobile requirements.

  • Government & public agencies

    State, county, city, and local government departments delivering information and services.

  • Public education

    Public schools, community colleges, universities, and their digital learning and service experiences.

  • Health & community services

    Public hospitals, clinics, benefits programs, and social services.

  • Transit & public spaces

    Transit authorities, libraries, parks, and recreation programs.

  • Courts & civic participation

    Courts, election offices, law enforcement, and public participation tools.

  • Special district governments

    Public entities such as water or fire districts, with the rule’s specific deadline treatment.

WHAT THE RULE REQUIRES

A technical standard.
A usable public experience.

The rule sets WCAG 2.1 Level A and AA success criteria and conformance requirements as its baseline. Testing needs to follow complete service journeys, including documents and relevant third-party components.

DOJ TECHNICAL BASELINEWCAG 2.1 AA

Level A + Level AA, with the conformance requirements.

Read the W3C standard

  • Keyboard & focus

    Operate controls without a mouse, follow a logical focus order, and see where focus is located.

  • Structure & labels

    Use meaningful headings, field labels, and programmatic relationships.

  • Text alternatives

    Provide useful alternatives for meaningful images and other non-text content.

  • Accessible media

    Provide captions, audio description, or text alternatives as the applicable media criteria require.

  • Contrast & perception

    Make text and essential interface details distinguishable; do not rely on color alone.

  • Reflow & zoom

    Support text resizing and reflow without losing information or required functionality.

  • Forms & feedback

    Make instructions and errors understandable, and expose relevant status updates.

  • Controls & interactions

    Provide accessible names, roles, and states, with appropriate gesture, timing, and motion behavior.

These are examples, not a complete checklist. W3C’s conformance requirements include full pages and complete processes. Broader WCAG 2.2 AA testing can be scoped while preserving the DOJ’s WCAG 2.1 mapping.

Exceptions need a closer look—not a blanket assumption.

The rule has five content exceptions, each with conditions. Password protection alone does not exempt a portal, website, or application.

  • Archived web contentQualifying older content kept unchanged, only for reference, research, or recordkeeping, in an area identified as an archive.
  • Pre-existing documentsCertain files available before the deadline, except documents currently used to access or participate in public services.
  • Independent third-party contentSome posts by third parties; not content posted through a contractual, licensing, or other arrangement with the public entity.
  • Individual secured documentsCertain conventional electronic documents about an individual, their property, or account, protected by a password or other security.
  • Pre-existing social media postsPosts published by the public entity before its compliance date.

Existing obligations for effective communication and equal participation continue. Your legal or compliance advisers should determine how an exception applies to particular content.

Read all five exceptions in 28 CFR § 35.201 · DOJ examples and explanations.

OUR FIVE-STEP PROCESS

From an accessibility backlog
to documented progress.

A practical sequence with clear handoffs. Your timeline depends on the content, technology, access, vendor dependencies, and people available to implement changes.

01 · Assess

Start with scope and priorities.

Identify the websites, documents, public-service journeys, and vendor dependencies involved. Agree the technical target, access requirements, testing coverage, responsibilities, and delivery milestones. Your legal advisers confirm legal applicability and the relevant deadline.

WHAT THIS STAGE PRODUCES
  • Digital asset and journey inventory
  • Agreed assessment scope
  • Responsibilities and project milestones

02 · Manual Testing

Understand the barriers in real use.

Use automated findings as one input, then investigate keyboard operation, screen-reader behavior, forms, navigation, and critical transactions through expert manual testing. Prioritize reproducible issues by user impact and service importance.

WHAT THIS STAGE PRODUCES
  • Reproduction steps and evidence
  • Relevant WCAG criteria
  • Prioritized technical findings

03 · Code-Level Remediation

Fix the underlying implementation.

Address agreed issues in code, content, and components. We can implement changes, guide your developers, and coordinate vendor-owned work. Track dependencies and unresolved findings rather than treating them as complete.

WHAT THIS STAGE PRODUCES
  • Scoped fixes and developer guidance
  • Issue ownership and status
  • Implementation notes

04 · Retest & Verify

Repeat the affected journeys.

Retest agreed fixes in the relevant environment using appropriate manual, keyboard, assistive-technology, and automated checks. Record whether each finding is resolved, still open, or requires further investigation.

WHAT THIS STAGE PRODUCES
  • Issue-level retest results
  • Regression and journey checks
  • Remaining limitations

05 · Documentation

Make the progress reviewable.

Bring scope, findings, remediation, and verification evidence into a usable technical record. Include an ACR, role-specific training, or an ongoing support plan where those deliverables are part of the engagement.

WHAT THIS STAGE PRODUCES
  • Findings and remediation record
  • Verification evidence and summary
  • Optional ACR and handoff materials

The full sequence applies when the engagement includes remediation and retesting. Audit-only, training, and advisory projects cover the agreed stages. Ongoing support can continue after the initial handoff.

THE RIGHT REQUIREMENT, IN CONTEXT

Connected standards.
Different legal scopes.

Title II is one part of the accessibility landscape. Your organization may need to account for more than one requirement.

Official references: DOJ Title II fact sheet · Current web and mobile regulation · W3C WCAG 2.1.

  • WCAG Compliance & Conformance

    Technical criteria, testing scope, and a clear conformance target.

  • ADA Website Compliance

    Website accessibility in the separate context of businesses open to the public.

  • Section 508 Compliance

    Accessibility of information and communication technology in the federal context.

  • Section 504 Compliance

    Digital accessibility under the HHS rule for recipients of HHS financial assistance.

Equal Access provides digital accessibility services, not legal advice.

We provide technical findings, remediation, and verification evidence. Your legal or compliance advisers determine legal obligations, exceptions, and response strategy. A report or ACR is not an ADA certification or a guarantee against claims.
