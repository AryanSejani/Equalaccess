Clarity before action

A claim has been made. Now understand the website behind it.

An accessibility complaint may identify barriers in specific pages, features, or user journeys. The technical question goes beyond a scanner’s results:

“Did a scanner find errors?”
“What accessibility barriers exist, where do they occur, and what needs to be remediated?”

We combine automated analysis and expert manual testing against relevant WCAG accessibility criteria.

We help answer

What barriers were reported?

We map the reported issues to the relevant pages, components, and user journeys.

Can they be reproduced?

We investigate how the reported barriers affect keyboard and assistive-technology use.

What else needs technical review?

We check whether related pages or interactions need a broader assessment.

What should be prioritized?

We weigh severity, user impact, affected functionality, and remediation effort.

The complaint may not tell the whole story.

Reported barriers can be a useful starting point, but additional accessibility issues may exist elsewhere on the website.

We can review the reported issues first, then help determine whether a broader accessibility assessment makes sense.

Five steps. One clear path.

From accessibility claim
to documented remediation.

01 — Assess

Understand what has been alleged

We map the reported accessibility barriers to the relevant pages, components, and user journeys to establish a starting point for testing.

What we review may include:

  • Pages and user journeys referenced in the claim
  • Alleged accessibility barriers
  • Forms, navigation, menus, and interactive components
  • Product, checkout, account, or booking experiences
  • Third-party functionality
  • Existing accessibility tools or overlays

A clear starting point for technical testing.

02 — Manual Testing

Verify the website through expert accessibility testing

Automated analysis and expert manual testing help us verify reported barriers and identify additional issues affecting website use.

Testing may include:

  • Keyboard access, focus order, and visibility
  • Screen-reader compatibility and ARIA
  • Forms, labels, and error handling
  • Page structure, headings, and image alternatives
  • Contrast, navigation, dialogs, and interactive controls
  • E-commerce and critical transactional journeys

The result: reproducible findings and a prioritized remediation plan.

Turn findings into a remediation plan

We prioritize barriers by user impact, severity, and implementation effort. Each finding gives your team a practical starting point.

03 — Code-Level Remediation

Fix accessibility barriers at the source

We address barriers in the underlying website, either directly or alongside your developers. Any limits on third-party access are documented.

Depending on your technology stack and project scope, remediation can include changes to:

  • HTML structure and CSS presentation
  • JavaScript and interactive components
  • Forms and navigation
  • Content and images
  • Themes, templates, and e-commerce functionality
  • Third-party integrations where source access allows

Code-level remediation. Clear priorities. Practical implementation.

04 — Retest & Verify

Retest the work

We retest affected pages, components, and user journeys to verify that the identified barriers have been addressed.

This may include:

  • Manual, keyboard, and screen-reader testing
  • Automated rescanning
  • Regression and browser/device checks
  • Critical user journey verification

Retest the changes. Verify the outcome.

05 — Documentation

Maintain a clear record of the accessibility work

We record the findings, fixes, verification results, and remaining limitations so progress is clear.

Documentation can include:

  • Audit findings and relevant WCAG criteria
  • Remediation priorities and issue status
  • Completed fixes and retesting results
  • Technical limitations and recommendations
  • Ongoing monitoring records where in scope

Your legal team can use the technical information as appropriate when managing the legal matter.

What you receive.

A complete technical accessibility workflow—from initial testing through remediation, verification, and documentation. Deliverables are agreed within your project scope.

Automated Test

A layered automated assessment helps identify detectable accessibility errors, WCAG gaps, structural issues, and areas requiring deeper review.

Testing details

Testing may include WAVE, Google Lighthouse, and additional accessibility validation tools. Automated results guide further review.

Manual Expert Audit

An accessibility expert manually reviews important pages, interactions, and user journeys that automated tools cannot fully evaluate.

Audit coverage
  • Keyboard navigation and screen-reader use
  • Forms and interactive components
  • Browser and device behavior

Where applicable, testing includes VoiceOver, NVDA, or JAWS.

Code-Level Remediation

Accessibility barriers are addressed within the underlying code, components, content, templates, and interactions wherever technically possible.

Implementation details

Work can include HTML, CSS, JavaScript, themes, forms, navigation, and interactive components.

Third-party integrations are addressed where technically accessible. Any source-access limitations that prevent a complete fix are documented.

Verification & Retesting

After remediation, affected pages, components, and important user journeys are retested to verify that identified barriers have been addressed.

Verification methods
  • Automated rescanning and manual retesting
  • Keyboard and screen-reader testing
  • Browser/device validation and regression checks

VPAT & Technical Reports

Receive clear documentation of the accessibility work performed, findings addressed, verification results, and any remaining technical limitations.

Reporting details
  • Before-and-after findings and remediation summaries
  • Resolved issues, testing results, and remaining limitations
  • VPAT / Accessibility Conformance Report when included in scope

We can cross-reference reported barriers against testing and remediation, giving your legal team organized technical information to review.

Video Verification

Where included in scope, an accessibility expert can provide a screen-recorded walkthrough of remediated user journeys and key functionality during testing.

Example walkthroughs
  • Keyboard navigation and screen-reader use
  • Forms, menus, and purchase or booking flows
  • Specific barriers referenced in the complaint

This provides an additional visual record of the testing and remediation work completed.

+ Developer coordination and ongoing monitoring are also available when needed.
