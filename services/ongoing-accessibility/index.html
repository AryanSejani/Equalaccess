THE RIGHT TOOL FOR THE RIGHT JOB

Monitoring catches change.
Expert testing tells you what it means.

Recurring checks provide useful signals. Manual evaluation is still needed to understand context, interactions, and how people complete important tasks.

Automated monitoring

Recurring checks for detectable issues.

  • Missing attributes and certain markup problems
  • Some contrast and ARIA failures
  • New or recurring findings across agreed pages

Manual review

Human judgment about the experience.

  • Meaningful text alternatives and labels
  • Keyboard operation and focus behavior
  • Screen-reader output and complete journeys

Retesting

Check the result after a change.

  • Repeat the affected interaction
  • Check keyboard and assistive-technology use
  • Review for regressions around the fix

THE RIGHT CADENCE

Set the review schedule
around how your website changes.

A website updated several times a week needs a different discussion from one that changes a few times a year.

Lower change

Stable informational websites.

Typical approach

Periodic reviews plus checks after major updates.

Moderate change

Service businesses, restaurants, and regular content publishing.

Typical approach

Scheduled monitoring and manual review around meaningful changes.

Higher change

E-commerce, frequent campaigns, and regular development releases.

Typical approach

More frequent automated checks plus recurring expert review of important journeys.

These are illustrative approaches, not universal testing requirements. We agree frequency around change activity, important functionality, user impact, and available resources.

ACCURATE EXPECTATIONS

Accessibility maintenance
continues with the website.

No scheduled scan or support plan can guarantee that a changing website remains free of every barrier at every moment. Automated tools also cannot determine full WCAG conformance on their own.

Monitor what tools can detect. Test what needs human judgment. Address the barriers found.

Our role is to investigate, remediate, verify, and document the agreed work. W3C’s evaluation overview explains why knowledgeable human evaluation matters.

Equal Access provides digital accessibility services, not legal advice.
