THE CONTEXT

A file can look clear.
Its structure can tell another story.

A report might look organized on screen while its headings, tables, and instructions are difficult to navigate with assistive technology. Accessibility depends on how the content is structured and how it behaves in use.

We help make that information understandable: meaningful headings, logical reading order, useful image descriptions, and forms people can complete.

A PRACTICAL EXAMPLE

In a product manual, a setup diagram needs an equivalent explanation. The instructions also need a sensible reading sequence and headings readers can navigate.

Choose the right target.

WCAG
Accessibility outcomes for content, applied to the relevant document format and requirements.
PDF/UA
PDF-specific technical requirements that complement WCAG. The appropriate version depends on the file and project.
Your publishing context
Audience, legal or contract requirements, reading software, source files, and intended use.

WHAT WE FIX

The details that make
a document usable.

Automated analysis helps locate detectable issues. Expert review determines the corrections that fit the document and its purpose.

Tags & structure

Identify headings, paragraphs, lists, tables, figures, and links with the appropriate document structure. Check table headers and relationships as well as the presence of tags.

Reading & tab order

Arrange content into a meaningful reading sequence. Review complex layouts and check the keyboard order of links and form fields in the agreed reading environment.

Images & graphics

Write text alternatives that convey the purpose of meaningful images. Provide fuller explanations for complex charts, and mark purely decorative content appropriately.

Forms & controls

Give interactive PDF fields meaningful accessible names, instructions, and an appropriate tab order. Review required-field information, errors, and completion behavior where applicable.

Contrast & color

Review text, essential graphics, and information conveyed by color. Correct presentation or add clear labels and other cues, with your approval where design changes are needed.

Language & metadata

Set useful document titles and language information, including language changes where supported. Review links, bookmarks, and navigation appropriate to the file.

Scanned documents may also require OCR, text correction, and rebuilding before structural remediation.

OUR FIVE-STEP PROCESS

From the first file
to a documented handover.

A clear sequence for understanding the collection, correcting barriers, and checking the results.

01 / Assess

Understand the document collection.

Review representative files, source formats, page counts, audiences, and publishing needs. Agree the accessibility target and identify priority forms, instructions, and frequently used documents.

PROJECT OUTPUT

Document inventory & agreed scope

File types, representative samples, target standards, and delivery requirements.

02 / Manual Testing

Look beyond the checker results.

Combine automated analysis with expert inspection of tags, reading order, tables, visuals, and interactive fields. Investigate how the files work in the agreed reader and assistive-technology environment, then prioritize the findings.

PROJECT OUTPUT

Findings & remediation plan

Document-level issues, affected pages, recommended fixes, and priorities.

03 / Code-Level Remediation

Repair the underlying structure.

Correct the document’s tags, content, properties, and form fields. Where editable sources are available, improve the source file and export settings before checking the resulting PDF. For this service, code-level work means the document’s underlying structure—not HTML overlays.

PROJECT OUTPUT

Remediated files

Agreed corrections to document content, semantic structure, and interaction.

04 / Retest & Verify

Check the finished experience.

Repeat automated checks and manually verify the affected content. Review reading order, heading navigation, tables, and forms with appropriate assistive technology. Record the tested environment and resolve or document remaining limitations.

PROJECT OUTPUT

Verification results

Retesting evidence tied to the delivered file version and agreed scope.

05 / Documentation

Deliver the files and the record.

Hand over the remediated documents with findings, completed work, test results, and any known limitations. Include authoring guidance and, when separately scoped, an Accessibility Conformance Report.

PROJECT OUTPUT

Organized handover

Final files, remediation status, verification notes, and next steps.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions
about document accessibility.

PDF remediation addresses accessibility barriers in a PDF’s content and underlying structure. It can include repairing tags, reading order, headings, tables, text alternatives, links, and form fields. The file is then evaluated against the agreed accessibility target; remediation alone is not a certification.

WCAG criteria can be applied to documents, with interpretation appropriate to the format and applicable requirements. For example, Revised Section 508 applies WCAG 2.0 Level A and AA to covered electronic content, with specific exceptions and adaptations for non-web documents. A document’s intended use and applicable requirements determine the target.

We agree the standard, version, level, file formats, and testing environment before starting. Work may be evaluated against WCAG 2.1 or 2.2 Level AA, applicable Section 508 criteria, and an appropriate PDF/UA target for PDFs. We do not assume every document is subject to the same legal requirements.

Automated checks help find detectable technical issues, but cannot determine whether every description is meaningful or every reading sequence makes sense. EqualAccess. combines tools with expert inspection, manual remediation, and assistive-technology testing. A passed checker does not establish complete accessibility.

Timing depends on file count, page count, complexity, source-file availability, and review requirements. Scanned pages, complex tables, and interactive forms can require additional work. We establish the scope and schedule after reviewing representative files, and can prioritize critical documents within the agreed plan.

WCAG defines accessibility outcomes across content. PDF/UA is a PDF-specific standard focused on the format’s accessibility features, including its logical structure. They are complementary: PDF/UA conformance alone does not establish that every relevant WCAG requirement, such as color contrast, has been met.

No. Technical remediation does not guarantee a legal outcome or prevent future claims. We focus on addressing identified barriers, verifying the agreed work, and documenting findings and limitations; qualified legal counsel should advise on legal obligations and risk.

Optical character recognition (OCR) can recover text from scanned pages, but the results need review and correction. Structure, reading order, tables, and text alternatives still need attention after OCR. Low-quality originals may need to be replaced or recreated, which we discuss during scoping.

Check new and revised documents before publishing, and retest when edits or exports may affect accessibility. The review schedule for an existing collection depends on how frequently it changes and how it is used. We can support recurring reviews and help your team build accessibility into its publishing workflow.

Additional guidance: Section508.gov document resources · Revised Section 508 content requirements · W3C guidance on scanned PDFs.
