ACCESSIBILITY AUDITS & TESTING

Find the accessibility barriers that actually stop people from using your website.

EqualAccess. combines automated analysis with expert manual testing, keyboard evaluation, assistive technology, and real user journeys to uncover problems a scan alone cannot explain.

MORE THAN AUTOMATION

A scanner helps find issues.
It cannot evaluate the whole experience.

Tools quickly flag detectable problems. Human evaluation adds the context needed to understand barriers and turn potential issues into useful findings.

AUTOMATED ANALYSIS

Find technical patterns.

Detectable markup problems, some contrast failures, missing attributes, certain ARIA issues, and repeated patterns across pages.

MANUAL TESTING

Understand the behavior.

Keyboard usability, focus order, meaningful labels, alternative-text quality, forms, dynamic interactions, context, and intent.

ASSISTIVE TECHNOLOGY

Check what is communicated.

Accessible names, roles, states, screen-reader announcements, dynamic updates, and navigation behavior.

USER JOURNEYS

Follow the complete task.

Can someone browse, choose, submit, book, or buy using the important flows on your website?

VALIDATED FINDINGS

A useful audit explains barriers—not just error counts.

W3C explains that knowledgeable human evaluation is required; no tool alone determines accessibility. Read W3C’s evaluation guidance.

THE EVALUATION

We test the parts of your website
people actually have to use.

The scope depends on your platform, important functionality, and the technical accessibility standard agreed for the engagement.

Structure & semantics

Headings, landmarks, lists, tables, page language, relationships, and semantic HTML.

Keyboard access

Tab order, operation, traps, shortcuts, menus, controls, forms, and interactive functionality.

Focus

Visible focus, logical movement, focus loss, dialogs, navigation changes, and dynamic content.

Forms

Labels, instructions, required fields, validation, errors, status messages, autocomplete, and submission.

Navigation

Main and mobile menus, dropdowns, skip mechanisms, links, search, breadcrumbs, and consistency.

Images & visual content

Text alternatives, decorative content, icons, charts, visual instructions, and information conveyed by appearance.

Color & presentation

Text and non-text contrast, text spacing, zoom, reflow, orientation, and color-only information.

Interactive components

Accordions, tabs, modals, carousels, filters, product selectors, tooltips, widgets, and dynamic states.

Media

Captions, transcripts, audio-description considerations, controls, and prerecorded content where applicable.

Assistive technology

Names, roles, states, reading order, screen-reader output, dynamic announcements, and component behavior.

Responsive experiences

Desktop and mobile behavior within the agreed scope.

Important user journeys

Checkout, registration, accounts, reservations, booking, quote requests, search, applications, and contact forms.

Inspect more of the website,
then put the results in context.

Depending on scope, we may combine multiple tools and browser checks to identify patterns across pages, templates, and components.

  • Missing accessible names
  • Certain contrast failures
  • Invalid or inappropriate ARIA
  • Missing form relationships
  • Some structural issues
  • Missing image alternatives
  • Markup problems affecting accessibility
  • Repeated template patterns

We don’t paste scanner output into a spreadsheet and call it an audit.

MANUAL EXPERT TESTING

Some of the most important questions
require a person.

We interact with navigation, forms, components, content, and workflows to identify barriers that depend on behavior, context, or human judgment.

Keyboard-only navigation
Can each important control be reached and operated?
Focus order
Does focus move in a logical, predictable sequence?
Visible focus
Can a keyboard user tell where they are?
Content structure
Do headings, landmarks, tables, and relationships communicate meaning?
Alternative text
Does it communicate the image’s purpose, rather than merely exist?
Forms
Are labels, instructions, errors, and state changes understandable?
Dynamic components
Do menus, tabs, filters, and dialogs behave appropriately?
Zoom & reflow
Does the experience remain usable when enlarged or reflowed?

SMART SCOPING

Comprehensive testing doesn’t mean
repeating the same page thousands of times.

Large websites often reuse templates, navigation, forms, and other components. We build a representative scope around meaningful differences and important journeys.

An illustrative 5,000-page store does not automatically need 5,000 separate manual page audits. Shared patterns can be evaluated together, while unique content, page states, and complete multi-step processes need consideration.

Testing scope is agreed before the work begins and clearly recorded in the findings.

Read W3C’s WCAG evaluation methodology overview.

HOW IT WORKS

From website review
to a clear remediation plan.

Our five-step framework connects testing to the work that follows. An audit provides assessment, expert testing, and documentation; implementation and post-fix verification are scoped separately.

  1. 01

    Assess

    Define the scope. Inspect the patterns.

    Agree pages, templates, components, journeys, environments, and the target standard. Automated analysis identifies candidates for further review.

    OUTPUTDefined scope and initial technical findings

  2. 02

    Manual Testing

    Validate the actual barriers.

    Combine manual, keyboard, assistive-technology, visual, and journey testing. Prioritize findings by impact, recurrence, and remediation needs.

    OUTPUTValidated barriers and a prioritized roadmap

  3. 03

    Code-Level Remediation

    Turn findings into technical changes.

    Your developer can implement the recommendations, or EqualAccess. can remediate where code access and a separate agreed scope allow.

    OUTPUTImplementation, when commissioned

  4. 04

    Retest & Verify

    Confirm the effect of the changes.

    After remediation, repeat the relevant tests and affected journeys when verification is included.

    OUTPUTVerification status for the retested findings

  5. 05

    Documentation

    Keep an actionable record.

    The audit report records the scope, evidence, impact, WCAG references, and guidance. Add remediation and verification updates as work progresses.

    OUTPUTAudit findings and subsequent technical records

Your audit report is delivered when the evaluation is complete; it does not wait for remediation. Need us to fix the issues too? Explore Code-Level Remediation.

ACTIONABLE FINDINGS

Your developer shouldn’t have to guess
what the report means.

Every meaningful finding should answer the questions needed to understand and act on it.

What is wrong?
A plain-language explanation of the barrier.
Where is it?
The page, template, component, or interaction.
Who can it affect?
The affected user experience.
How was it found?
The relevant automated, manual, keyboard, or assistive-technology method.
Which requirement?
The applicable WCAG criterion or agreed technical requirement.
How should it change?
Practical remediation guidance.
Has it been fixed?
Remediation and retesting status when verification is included.

NOT EVERY FINDING HAS THE SAME IMPACT

Start with barriers that
affect important experiences.

Prioritization gives your team an order of work. We consider impact, reach, implementation effort, and the dependencies needed to address each finding.

User impact
Does the barrier block access to content or prevent an action?
Journey importance
Does it affect checkout, account access, booking, or another important task?
Recurrence
Is it isolated, or repeated through a shared template or component?
Technical severity
How does the implementation fail the applicable requirement?
Remediation efficiency
Could one component change address repeated barriers?
Third-party control
Can your team change the code, or is a vendor involved?

ACCURATE EXPECTATIONS

An audit tells you what needs attention.
It doesn’t fix the website by itself.

An audit is an evaluation. If it identifies barriers, remediation is still needed. A scanner score or audit report is not a permanent guarantee for a website that keeps changing.
