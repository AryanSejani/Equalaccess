THE TEMPLATE. THE EVALUATION. THE REPORT.

The VPAT® is the template.
The ACR is the report.

The terms are often used interchangeably. Understanding the difference makes it easier to know what you are asking for—and what your customer needs.

VPAT®

Voluntary Product Accessibility Template

A standardized template developed by the Information Technology Industry Council (ITI) for documenting how information and communications technology supports applicable accessibility requirements.

The structure used to organize and report the evaluation.

EVALUATE
& DOCUMENT

ACR

Accessibility Conformance Report

Once the VPAT® has been completed for a specific product using actual evaluation results, the finished document is an Accessibility Conformance Report.

The report you can share with customers, procurement teams, government buyers, and other organizations evaluating your product.

You do not simply “get a VPAT.” A useful ACR starts with an actual evaluation of the product it describes.

Read ITI’s VPAT® explanation and GSA’s guide to preparing an ACR.

MORE THAN A FORM

An ACR is only as useful as the evaluation behind it.

Completing a template without evaluating the product can produce vague or inaccurate documentation. Our approach starts with the actual product: test first, document second.

THE EVALUATION

We test the product
behind the report.

The evaluation depends on your product, the relevant VPAT® edition, and the requirements being documented. We agree the product scope, environments, workflows, and methods before testing begins.

  • Automated accessibility testing

    Tools identify detectable issues and provide technical signals for deeper evaluation.

  • Keyboard testing

    Can important functionality be reached, operated, and understood without a mouse?

  • Screen reader testing

    Relevant interfaces and workflows are evaluated with assistive technology.

  • Forms & validation

    Labels, instructions, required fields, errors, status messages, and interaction behavior.

  • Navigation & focus

    Focus order, visible focus, menus, dialogs, navigation patterns, and other controls.

  • Structure & semantics

    Headings, landmarks, names, roles, states, relationships, tables, and programmatic information.

  • Visual accessibility

    Color contrast, text presentation, reflow, zoom, non-text content, and related requirements.

  • Core user journeys

    Authentication, account management, search, purchasing, applications, and other important tasks.

A scanner alone cannot establish accessibility conformance. W3C describes testing as a combination of automated checks and human evaluation. Read W3C’s conformance guidance.

do you need?

The appropriate edition depends on the requirements your customer, procurement process, or market needs documented. ITI currently provides four VPAT® 2.5Rev editions.

EditionWhat it coversCommon fit

VPAT® WCAGWCAG Edition

COVERSApplicable Web Content Accessibility Guidelines criteria. The current edition can address WCAG 2.0, 2.1, and 2.2.

COMMON FITCustomers requesting WCAG-based accessibility documentation.

VPAT® 508Revised Section 508

COVERSThe Revised Section 508 standards for U.S. federal information and communications technology.

COMMON FITVendors and products involved in U.S. federal procurement.

VPAT® EUEU Edition

COVERSEN 301 549 accessibility requirements for information and communications technology.

COMMON FITProducts needing documentation aligned with applicable European ICT requirements.

VPAT® INTInternational Edition

COVERSThe standards represented by the WCAG, Revised Section 508, and EU editions in a broader report.

COMMON FITProducts serving multiple markets or procurement environments.

CLEAR CONFORMANCE REPORTING

A useful ACR explains
more than “pass” or “fail.”

The VPAT® reporting structure uses defined terms to describe how the evaluated product supports individual requirements, with space for remarks and explanations.

  • Supports

    A method meets the criterion without known defects, or equivalent facilitation is provided.

  • Partially Supports

    Some functionality supports the criterion, while some does not.

  • Does Not Support

    The majority of the evaluated functionality does not meet the criterion.

  • Not Applicable

    The criterion does not apply to the evaluated product or functionality.

Supports

REMARKS & EXPLANATIONS

The evaluated navigation, forms, dialogs, and date-selection workflow can be operated using a keyboard. Testing found no keyboard-only operation barriers in the functionality covered by this criterion.

Illustrative remarks for a hypothetical product. The full evaluation and applicable reporting instructions determine the actual finding.

CONFORMANCE LEVEL

Partially Supports

REMARKS & EXPLANATIONS

Most primary navigation and form controls are keyboard operable. One custom date-picker component does not currently expose all functionality through keyboard interaction.

The remarks identify the specific limitation, instead of leaving the reader to guess what is affected.

CONFORMANCE LEVEL

Does Not Support

REMARKS & EXPLANATIONS

The majority of functionality in the evaluated workspace relies on pointer interaction. Keyboard users cannot operate the primary navigation, open key dialogs, or complete the core scheduling workflow.

The explanation describes affected functionality and the practical impact on the user.

The explanation matters.

Useful remarks show what was tested, where limitations exist, and how the product behaves. These simplified examples demonstrate reporting quality; they are not EqualAccess. client findings.

AN IMPORTANT DISTINCTION

A VPAT® is not an
accessibility certification.

There is no official “VPAT certification.” ITI provides the template; it does not approve or certify individual VPATs or ACRs.

Our role is to help you evaluate accurately, document clearly, and remediate barriers where appropriate. The result is useful accessibility information about the product evaluated.

Read ITI’s explanation of VPAT® reporting.

STANDARDS & PROCUREMENT

How WCAG and Section 508 fit into the picture.

WCAG

W3C’s Web Content Accessibility Guidelines define testable success criteria at Levels A, AA, and AAA. WCAG is a technical standard; laws or contracts may reference it.

WCAG Compliance & Conformance

Section 508

Section 508 addresses accessibility of ICT developed, procured, maintained, or used by U.S. federal agencies. Vendors commonly use an ACR to document support for the applicable requirements.
